4 · Role Playing

Every role asks for a different kind of confidence.

Observer

Viewer

“Keep me close to the plan.”

  • See daily activities and locations
  • Accurate, complete, up-to-date itinerary
  • Future PDF export
  • Nothing financial, no reports
Co-planner

Editor

“Let me contribute without damage.”

  • Edit itinerary independently
  • See and edit money and budget items
  • Clear signal that controls are editable
  • Cannot delete trip or share it
Steward

Manager

“Make access safe and easy.”

  • Invite, remove, change access
  • Delete trip with full access
  • See fresh changes from others
  • Trust server-enforced roles

5 · Assumption Reversal

Turning defaults inside out clarified privacy and duration.

Tempting reversal

“Let people request access.”

Useful only if trips become publicly discoverable—an avoidable privacy risk for a private-trip feature.

↔
Chosen principle

Managers issue invitations.

Contributor access persists after trip end until a manager removes it. Private-by-default is trust boundary.

6 · Failure Analysis

Stale shared information is catastrophic. Freshness is feature, not polish.

Live, without reload.

Changes must appear across sessions as they happen. Simultaneous edits resolve as latest-write-wins.

Sync stops?Status bar shows offline / internet-down state.
Access revoked live?Modal names trip owner, directs contact, and returns recipient to dashboard; back cannot reopen trip.
Wrong person or role?Manager adjusts role or removes contributor in share dialog.
